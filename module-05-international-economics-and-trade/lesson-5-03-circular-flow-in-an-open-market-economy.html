📚 PART 1 · FIVE SECTORS, ONE CONTINUOUS LOOP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think about your own household. In the last month, can you name a way money flowed out to a firm, and a way it flowed back in from one (a paycheck, a return, anything)? (No wrong answer.)

Households and Firms: The Core Loop

Households supply the factors of production (land, labor, capital, entrepreneurship) to firms through the factor market, earning wages, rent, interest, and profit in return. Firms turn those inputs into goods and services, which households buy back through the product market using the income they just earned. This reciprocal loop — resources out, income in, income out, goods in — is the engine at the center of everything else in this lesson.

Government's Role

Government is multifaceted: it collects taxes from households (income, sales) and firms (corporate, property, excise), then reinjects that money as salaries, purchases from firms, infrastructure investment, and transfer payments. Spending raises aggregate demand directly; transfer payments reduce disparities by supporting households whose private income falls short. Fiscal policy can also act countercyclically, smoothing the business cycle rather than just moving money.

Financial Institutions: The Connector

Banks and other financial institutions collect household savings and channel them to firms and governments needing capital. Households earn interest, dividends, or capital gains on what they save, which then re-enters the loop as spending. This is the mechanism that connects saving today to investment and growth tomorrow — without it, resources would sit idle instead of funding new production.

International Trade: Extending the Loop Globally

Exports bring foreign revenue into the domestic loop; imports let households and firms access what's produced more efficiently elsewhere. Exchange rates and trade policy shape how big and in what direction these multidirectional flows run. This is where comparative advantage from Lesson 5.01 and the trade barriers from 5.02 physically show up inside the circular flow diagram.

📚 KEY VOCABULARY

Circular Flow Model — a representation of how money, goods, and resources move continuously between sectors of an economy.
Factor Market — where households provide land, labor, and capital to firms in exchange for income.
Compensation — payment households receive for supplying factors of production.
Managerial — relating to organizing, directing, and controlling a business.
Product Market — where firms' goods and services are sold to households, businesses, or government.
Reciprocal — involving mutual exchange between two parties.
Multifaceted — having many different aspects or dimensions.
Aggregate Demand — the total demand for goods and services in an economy at a given price level.
Disparity — a significant difference or inequality between things.
Countercyclically — policies that move opposite to the current business-cycle phase to stabilize the economy.
Capital Gains — profit earned from selling an asset above its purchase price.
Multidirectional — moving in several directions at once.
Exchange Rates — the value of one currency relative to another.
Vulnerability — susceptibility to harm, loss, or disruption.
Reverberate — to produce a continuing, spreading effect.
Complementary — something that enhances or completes another element.
Cascade — a process where one change triggers a sequence of others.
Static — unchanging or fixed.
